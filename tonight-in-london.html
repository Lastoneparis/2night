Tonight in London: a one-night dating app for a city of villages

By the 2NIGHT team · Updated 7 September 2026

2NIGHT is a one-night dating app for iPhone that shows who is out near you tonight, so you can match and meet the same evening instead of chatting for days.

London is not one night out; it is thirty of them happening at once, ten Tube stops apart. That is exactly the problem a radius solves. Instead of matching with someone in Walthamstow while you sit in a pub in Clapham, 2NIGHT shows you the people who are live tonight within a distance you choose, so a “fancy a drink?” can actually become a drink before the last train.

How a night out works in London

The first thing to understand about London is how early it starts and how early most of it ends. After-work drinks begin around 6 pm and the pubs are at their fullest between 7 and 9. Most pubs call last orders around 11 pm; bars run later and clubs later still, but on a Tuesday the realistic window for meeting someone new is roughly 7 to 11. On Fridays and Saturdays the city stretches: dinner at 8, a bar until 1, and in Dalston, Peckham or Brixton the night can run well past that.

The second thing is that Londoners go out near where they work on weeknights and near where they live at weekends. A Wednesday evening pulls people to Soho, Fitzrovia, London Bridge, Farringdon and Shoreditch, close to the office. A Saturday pulls them to their own patch: Clapham and Brixton south of the river, Hackney, Dalston and Stoke Newington to the north-east, Peckham to the south-east, Camden and Islington in the north, Notting Hill and Hammersmith to the west. Go live on a Saturday from a central hotel and the feed can look quieter than on Wednesday; the people are still out, just spread across the villages.

The third thing is the river. The Thames divides London more than any map suggests. A 3 km radius drawn from Soho reaches Waterloo and Southwark comfortably, but for a spontaneous meeting most people will not cross from Clapham to Camden on a weeknight. Set your radius with the river in mind and lead with a specific area in your status line: “Shoreditch, free from 8” gets more replies than “London, free tonight”.

Radius settings that make sense in London

2NIGHT uses a radius around your location and a nightly “Tonight” status that expires at 5 AM; your profile only appears in the feed while you are live, next to other people who are out that night. The max-distance slider starts at 1 km and is capped at 5 km on the free Silver tier, 15 km on Gold and 50 km on Platinum Black. In London those numbers mean something concrete:

  • 1–3 km: one neighbourhood and its neighbours. From Soho this covers Covent Garden, Fitzrovia, Marylebone and the Southbank; from Shoreditch it covers Hoxton, Dalston, Bethnal Green and the City. Everything is walkable or one bus away.
  • 5 km (Silver cap): most of Zone 1 plus a slice of Zone 2 in one direction. From King's Cross that reaches Camden, Islington, Clerkenwell and Bloomsbury. It is the right setting for a weeknight when you want a match you can reach in fifteen minutes.
  • 15 km (Gold): roughly Zones 1 to 4 in every direction. Useful on a slow evening or if you live out in Zone 3 and would happily travel in, but expect a match at that distance to cost you forty minutes each way.
  • 50 km (Platinum Black): the whole of Greater London and beyond. It is there for the quiet nights and for people outside the city; on a Friday in Zone 1 it is far more than you need.

Greater London is about 50 km across, so even the largest radius does not turn the city into a single feed. A same-night date is a date you can physically get to.

Last Tube, Night Tube and getting home

Transport shapes the whole evening. On Sunday to Thursday nights the last Tube trains leave central London around midnight to half past, and the last thing you want is to be three stops from a good conversation when the platforms close. On Friday and Saturday nights the Night Tube runs through the night on the Central, Jubilee, Northern, Piccadilly and Victoria lines, plus part of the Overground, which is why weekend nights in Brixton, Dalston or Stratford can run so late. Night buses (the N routes) run every night of the week on most main roads, so a match a few kilometres away is never really stranded; it is just slower. Black cabs and app taxis fill the gaps. Practically: on a weeknight, suggest a place near a line that serves both of you, agree a time before 9, and let the last train be the natural end of the evening.

Weeknight versus weekend on 2NIGHT

Because the “Tonight” status expires at 5 AM and the chat window lasts 48 hours, the app fits the London week rather than fighting it. On a weeknight, go live around 6 with a category (drinks, food, talk, walk) and a one-line status naming the area you are in; ping early, because the useful window closes by 11. At the weekend, the later you go live the more the feed shifts to nightlife and music, and a Plan (“pub garden in Peckham from 8, come say hi”) is often the best way to pull two or three people together instead of hoping for one perfect match. Safety on a same-night date in London

2NIGHT does not verify identities or photos and runs no background checks, so the habits on our safety page do the work: meet in a busy, well-lit public place, never a private home or a car; tell a friend where you are going and who you are meeting; keep your own way home; never send money, gift cards or crypto to anyone you met in the app. In London that mostly means picking a pub or bar near a station you both know, and keeping an eye on the last train so nobody is pressured into a lift. Any match can start a two-minute peer-to-peer video call before you leave, free during the launch period. Block is instant, silent and mutual; reports (inappropriate content, fake or scam, harassment) are read by a person within 24 to 72 hours. Other members only ever see a rounded distance in kilometres, never your address.

What radius should I set in London?

On a weeknight, 3 to 5 km from wherever you are keeps every match within a short bus or Tube ride. The free tier is capped at 5 km, Gold at 15 km and Platinum Black at 50 km; Greater London is about 50 km across.

Does the Tube run all night in London?

Only on Friday and Saturday nights, and only on the Central, Jubilee, Northern, Piccadilly and Victoria lines plus part of the Overground. Other nights the last trains leave central London around midnight to half past; night buses run every night.

Which areas of London are busiest on a weeknight?

Soho, Fitzrovia, London Bridge, Farringdon and Shoreditch fill up after work. Weekends spread out to Clapham, Brixton, Dalston, Hackney, Peckham, Camden and Islington.
