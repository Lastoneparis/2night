Making Friends in a New City: A Guide to Going Out Solo (and Staying Safe)

By the 2NIGHT team · 9 min read

Moving somewhere new is exciting for about a week. Then the boxes are unpacked, the novelty fades, and you realise you don't actually know anyone. Building a social circle from zero is one of the most underrated challenges of adult life — and one of the most doable, once you treat it as a skill rather than luck.

Why making friends as an adult feels hard

As kids we made friends by proximity and repetition — the same classmates, every day, for years. Adult life strips both away. People are scattered, schedules are full, and the easy “we just kept running into each other” path disappears. Nothing's wrong with you; the structure that used to do the work is simply gone.

Which means the fix is structural too. To make friends in a new city you need to manufacture the two ingredients childhood handed you for free: proximity (being physically near the same people) and repetition (seeing them more than once).

Go out solo — it's a feature, not a failure

Many people stay in simply because they have no one to go out with — and waiting for company is the single biggest reason newcomers stay isolated. But going out alone is quietly one of the best social tools you have. Without a friend to retreat into, you're far more approachable.

Make solo outings easy on yourself:

  • Pick the bar, not the table: sitting at a bar or counter invites conversation in a way a corner table never will.
  • Go where there's a shared activity: a class, a run club, a games night, a tasting — a built-in reason to talk to strangers.
  • Become a regular: the same café or gym three times a week turns strangers into familiar faces, then into friends.
  • Give yourself an exit: “I'll stay 45 minutes” removes the pressure and almost always turns into longer.

Turn one meeting into a circle

A social circle isn't built one friend at a time in isolation — it grows through connection. The person you meet tonight knows other people, hosts other plans, frequents other places. Your job is to convert single encounters into repeat ones.

One genuine evening, followed up on, is worth more than fifty introductions you never see again.
  • Follow up within a day or two — a specific “that bar was great, let's do the Thursday quiz next week” beats a vague “we should hang out.”
  • Say yes to the invitation behind the invitation — the house party, the friend's birthday, the group hike.
  • Be the one who organises. People are grateful to whoever sends the “anyone free Friday?” message. Be that person.

The going-out-solo safety checklist

Meeting new people is wonderful; doing it sensibly is non-negotiable, especially in a city you don't know well yet. None of this should make you anxious — treat it as the same kind of automatic habit as locking your door.

  • Meet in public, busy places. A first meet is always a bar, café or venue — never a private home, never an isolated spot.
  • Tell someone your plan. Share where you're going, who you're meeting and a rough timeline with a friend back home or new.
  • Keep your own way home. Don't rely on someone you just met for your ride. Know your route and keep enough battery and cash.
  • Mind your drink and your pace. Watch it being poured, keep it with you, and stay clear-headed enough to make good calls.
  • Guard personal details early. Your exact address, workplace and routine can wait until trust is earned.
  • Trust your gut. If anything feels off, you owe no one an explanation. Leaving early is always a complete sentence.

Give it time — and keep showing up

A real social circle in a new city usually takes a few months, not a few nights, and the people who build one fastest aren't the most charismatic — they're the ones who keep showing up. Every evening you go out is a small bet, and you only need a few to pay off.

The takeaway

Friendship in a new city is proximity plus repetition, engineered on purpose. Go out solo, use your radius to find who's near, follow up relentlessly, and keep your safety habits automatic. Show up enough times and the circle builds itself.
