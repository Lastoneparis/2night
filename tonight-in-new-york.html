Tonight in New York: a one-night dating app for a city that stays open

By the 2NIGHT team · Updated 7 September 2026

2NIGHT is a one-night dating app for iPhone that shows who is out near you tonight, so you can match and meet the same evening instead of chatting for days.

New York is the easiest city in the world to be out in and one of the hardest to actually meet someone in, because there is always somewhere else to be. A radius fixes the second part. 2NIGHT shows you the people who are live tonight within a distance you set, so “drinks?” is a question about the next hour and the next few blocks, not about which borough you might both be in some Thursday.

How a night out works in New York

The New York evening has a long tail. Happy hour runs from about 5 to 7, dinner is 7 to 9, and the bars fill up from 9 onwards. Because bars in New York State can serve until 4 am, a weeknight does not have the hard stop that London or Paris has; a Tuesday can end at midnight or at 3, depending entirely on the company. Weekends start later and go later, and in Bushwick or Williamsburg the real night often begins after midnight.

Where people go depends on the day. On weeknights Manhattan does most of the work: the East Village and Lower East Side for bars, the West Village for something quieter, Hell's Kitchen and Chelsea for the west side, Koreatown for late food, Harlem uptown. Weekends tilt to Brooklyn and Queens: Williamsburg and Greenpoint along the East River, Bushwick further out on the L, Bed-Stuy and Crown Heights, Astoria and Long Island City across the water in Queens. If you are staying in Midtown, note that it empties in the evening; going live from the East Village or Williamsburg puts you where people already are.

The other thing to know is the geography. Manhattan is a long island, roughly 21 km from tip to tip and under 4 km wide, with Brooklyn and Queens across the East River and the Bronx to the north. A radius that looks small on the map can cover a lot of the useful city, because the useful city is dense.

Radius settings that make sense in New York

2NIGHT uses a radius around your location and a nightly “Tonight” status that expires at 5 AM; your profile only appears in the feed while you are live, next to other people who are out that night. The max-distance slider starts at 1 km and is capped at 5 km on the free Silver tier, 15 km on Gold and 50 km on Platinum Black. In New York terms:

  • 1–2 km: your neighbourhood. From the East Village that is the Lower East Side, NoHo, Gramercy and the edge of the West Village, all walkable.
  • 5 km (Silver cap): most of Manhattan below Midtown plus Williamsburg and Greenpoint across the river. This is the setting for a weeknight when you want to meet within twenty minutes.
  • 15 km (Gold): all of Manhattan, most of Brooklyn and western Queens. Useful when you live further out or on a quiet Monday; at 15 km a match may be a forty-minute subway ride away.
  • 50 km (Platinum Black): the five boroughs and well into New Jersey, Long Island and Westchester. It exists for the quiet nights and for people outside the city, not for a Saturday in Brooklyn.

Lead with the neighbourhood in your status line. “LES, free from 9” tells someone in Astoria whether it is realistic; “NYC tonight” does not.

The subway never closes, but it slows down

New York's transport reality is the opposite of most European cities: the subway runs 24 hours a day, so there is no last train to plan around. What changes is frequency. After midnight trains come every twenty minutes or so on many lines, and weekend service changes and reroutes are common, so a “quick” trip across the river at 2 am can take an hour. Yellow cabs and app taxis are everywhere in Manhattan and northern Brooklyn; the ferries along the East River stop in the evening. Practically: on a weeknight, meet somewhere on a line that serves both of you and does not require a transfer late at night; at the weekend, check the service changes before you agree on Bushwick.

Weeknight versus weekend on 2NIGHT

The “Tonight” status expires at 5 AM, which in New York lines up neatly with last call at 4. On a weeknight, go live around 6 with a category (drinks, food, talk, walk, coffee) and a status that names your neighbourhood; pings sent before 9 have the best chance of turning into a plan. At weekends, the feed shifts toward nightlife and music the later you go live, and a Plan is often better than a one-to-one: “rooftop in Williamsburg from 10, come find us” brings a small group together and takes the pressure off. A mutual ping opens a 48-hour chat, so a Thursday match that did not work out can still become a Friday date.

Safety on a same-night date in New York

2NIGHT does not verify identities or photos and runs no background checks, so the habits on our safety page do the work: meet in a busy, well-lit public place, never a private home or a car; tell a friend where you are going and who you are meeting; keep your own way home; never send money, gift cards or crypto to anyone you met in the app. In New York that usually means a bar on a subway line you both use and a route home you have checked, since a 3 am transfer can be a long wait on an empty platform. Any match can start a two-minute peer-to-peer video call before you leave, free during the launch period. Block is instant, silent and mutual; reports (inappropriate content, fake or scam, harassment) are read by a person within 24 to 72 hours. Other members only ever see a rounded distance in kilometres, never your address.

Yes, 24 hours a day, but after midnight trains are much less frequent and weekend service changes are common. There is no last train, only a slower one.

The East Village, Lower East Side, West Village, Hell's Kitchen and Koreatown in Manhattan; Williamsburg and Greenpoint in Brooklyn. Weekends push further out to Bushwick, Bed-Stuy, Crown Heights and Astoria.
